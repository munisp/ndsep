# State-Specific Procurement Deck Outline

## Purpose

This outline defines three procurement-grade presentation variants for **Lagos**, **FCT**, and **Kano**, using the same platform backbone but adapting the narrative to each jurisdiction's land-administration priorities, Certificate of Occupancy workflow emphasis, geospatial review needs, and revenue-assurance logic.

## Shared Deck Backbone

Each state deck should keep a common structure so procurement audiences can compare the platform consistently. The deck should open with the state-specific land-administration challenge, explain what government is procuring, show the real platform screens, and then translate platform capability into service delivery, fiscal visibility, and implementation credibility.

| Slide theme | Shared message | State-specific emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Procurement-grade land and approvals operating platform | State name, relevant agencies, and procurement framing |
| Service problem | Fragmented land and approval systems weaken service and trust | Local land, titling, and development pressures |
| What government is procuring | Workflow backbone, not just a permit portal | Institutional fit with local registry and planning functions |
| Geospatial intelligence | Parcels, map context, corridor overlays, and field evidence | Local growth corridors, urban pressure, ROW, mining, or peri-urban expansion |
| C of O and legal workflow | Draft-to-registration continuity | Local title and regularisation pain points |
| Field operations | Low-connectivity continuity | Field conditions and verification realities |
| AI-assisted intake | Faster document handling with human verification | Volume and quality-control relevance |
| Governance and queues | Handoffs, reminders, escalations, overrides | Multi-desk discipline and agency accountability |
| Audit integrity | Signed packages, verification, custody, revocation | Defensibility and public trust |
| Revenue assurance | Traceability, formalisation visibility, reduced leakage | Local IGR and approval-linked value capture |
| Stakeholder value | Federal, state, builder, NGO, buyer value | Tailored stakeholder mix per state |
| Rollout case | Phased implementation and procurement readiness | Priority pilot path in the state |

## Lagos Deck

### Core framing

The Lagos version should emphasize **urban growth pressure, C of O throughput, builder coordination, right-of-way conflict review, and service speed in a dense commercial environment**. The main message is that Lagos needs a platform that improves the speed and defensibility of land administration while helping the state manage formalisation and development intensity more visibly.

### Most important slides

| Slide | Lagos-specific emphasis |
|---|---|
| Problem slide | High volume of development activity, title regularisation pressure, and the cost of slow land workflows |
| Geospatial slide | Housing growth corridors, infrastructure coordination, and parcel-based review in a dense urban market |
| C of O slide | Faster visibility into C of O progression, review bottlenecks, and citizen-facing service confidence |
| Revenue slide | More transparent land-linked workflow value, lower leakage risk, and better visibility into state value capture |
| Builder value slide | Faster project conversion, reduced idle capital, clearer approval sequencing, and stronger buyer confidence |

### Best screenshots

The Lagos deck should prioritize the **full-screen parcel map**, the **Geo screen with real web map**, the **C of O legal workflow**, and the **Permit Detail** view. These screens support the strongest visual story around parcel-driven urban approvals, formalisation continuity, and builder execution discipline.

## FCT Deck

### Core framing

The FCT version should emphasize **federal oversight, AGIS-style computerized cadastral management, area council land-document regularisation, mass-housing titling continuity, and national investment confidence**. The platform should be positioned as a way to connect title administration, large-scale housing delivery, and federal-quality audit trust under one operating model.[1]

### Most important slides

| Slide | FCT-specific emphasis |
|---|---|
| Problem slide | Fragmented document regularisation, mass-housing title issuance complexity, and visibility gaps across high-value districts |
| Geospatial slide | Capital-region parcel review, corridor-sensitive infrastructure, and map-backed desk oversight |
| Legal workflow slide | C of O, Right of Occupancy, and area-council regularisation visibility |
| Governance slide | Federal-grade queue discipline, escalations, and exception dashboards |
| Revenue slide | Stronger oversight of title-related obligations, reduced process opacity, and better investment-climate signals |

### Evidence anchors

The FCT deck can cite AGIS language that land decisions are taken from a more informed position with reliable and up-to-date data, and that AGIS is intended as a comprehensive geospatial data infrastructure for the FCT.[1] It can also reference official notices around mass-housing title issuance, reviewed terms for statutory rights of occupancy, and re-commencement of area-council land-document regularisation.[1]

## Kano Deck

### Core framing

The Kano version should emphasize **systematic land titling and registration, field demarcation, parcel digitisation, C of O generation, post-certificate transactions, and revenue modernization through GIS-backed land administration**. The key message is that the platform supports a state program that must combine field work, public display, registry discipline, and revenue-sensitive post-title activity.[2] [3]

### Most important slides

| Slide | Kano-specific emphasis |
|---|---|
| Problem slide | Legacy analogue land records, field data-collection needs, and pressure to simplify certification processes |
| Geospatial slide | Field demarcation, parcel digitisation, and GIS-backed land administration |
| C of O slide | SLTR-linked title progression, post-C of O transactions, and verification workflow visibility |
| Field slide | Data capture, evidence photography, public display, and low-connectivity mission continuity |
| Revenue slide | Ground-rent visibility, post-certificate transaction traceability, and new revenue-source support through GIS modernization |

### Evidence anchors

KANGIS describes itself as the source and custodian of geospatial data in Kano State and says it facilitates computerization of analogue land records, C of O generation, GIS backbone establishment, and new revenue sources for the state.[2] The SLTR unit further describes field operation, parcel digitization, C of O generation, post-C of O transactions, and ground-rent responsibilities.[3]

## Procurement Narrative Guidance

Each deck should avoid sounding like a technology brochure. The narrative should stay focused on **service delivery**, **title continuity**, **state land-administration discipline**, **fiscal visibility**, **investment confidence**, and **lower delay cost**. The geospatial map should be presented as evidence that the platform can tie place, parcel, and process together in one review environment, rather than as a generic mapping feature.
