# IDLR-PTS Stakeholder Presentation Script

This script is designed for a Nigeria-focused audience that includes **federal regulators, state land agencies, and builders**, with secondary resonance for NGOs, lenders, and end buyers. The tone should remain strategic, practical, and non-technical. The presenter should consistently frame the platform not as software for its own sake, but as a tool for **faster delivery, better accountability, stronger land certainty, and more trusted economic activity**.

## Slide 1 — Title

Begin by positioning IDLR-PTS as an integrated public-delivery platform rather than a narrow permit application. Explain that the platform brings together **land records, permits, parcel intelligence, field inspections, document verification, and audit integrity** in one operating environment. State clearly that the ambition is to make this the premier workflow platform for Nigeria because it addresses the real friction points that slow investment, housing delivery, extractives governance, and public trust.

## Slide 2 — Why Nigeria Needs This Now

Emphasize that Nigeria’s challenge is not simply demand for approvals; it is the fragmentation between agencies, field teams, evidence files, and location-based decision making. Explain that when land records, permit status, inspections, and supporting documents live in disconnected systems, the result is delay, opacity, avoidable dispute, and lost economic momentum. Position the platform as a practical response to those institutional gaps.

## Slide 3 — What the Platform Really Is

Explain that the platform is a **workflow operating system** for land-linked public and private delivery. It combines parcel intelligence, multi-agency approvals, AI-assisted document handling, low-connectivity field operations, notifications, and tamper-evident audit controls. Say that this matters in Nigeria because public delivery must work across federal institutions, state agencies, developers, communities, and buyers, not just inside one office.

## Slide 4 — Geospatial and Parcel Intelligence

This is one of the most important slides. State that in Nigeria, land, mining, rights-of-way, planning, and infrastructure approvals are fundamentally **place-based decisions**. The platform therefore links each case to parcels, coordinates, title context, workflow stage, GeoLibre readiness, and field triggers. Make clear that the **Geo screenshot shown here is a real web screenshot of the parcel-intelligence workflow**, while the richer live mobile map surface is available in native device builds. This distinction builds trust while still showing the geospatial architecture clearly.

## Slide 5 — Field Operations in Low-Connectivity Conditions

Explain that the platform is designed for the realities of Nigerian operations, where inspections, boundary checks, corridor reviews, and evidence capture often happen in areas with unstable connectivity. Highlight how missions can continue with offline continuity, local secure caching, and replay once service returns. For federal and state stakeholders, the key message is that the system does not break down the moment teams leave urban connectivity conditions.

## Slide 6 — AI-Assisted Document Extraction and Review

Frame AI here as a productivity and quality-control layer, not a replacement for officials. Explain that uploaded PDFs and images can be parsed to prefill forms, reducing repetitive manual entry, while side-by-side verification ensures the reviewer still compares extracted fields against the original evidence. This is valuable to regulators because it shortens intake time, to state agencies because it improves throughput, and to builders because it reduces rework and form resubmission.

## Slide 7 — Multi-Agency Permitting and Role-Based Governance

Explain that complex approvals fail most often at the handoff points between agencies, not at the form itself. The platform addresses this by structuring queues, roles, deadlines, reassignments, and escalation paths. Stress that the profile and queue view shown in the deck is a real screenshot of role-based operational governance. For federal institutions, this creates oversight; for state agencies, it creates accountability; for builders, it creates predictability.

## Slide 8 — Notifications, Handoffs, and Supervisor Follow-Through

Explain that many public workflows become slow because important tasks are visible only in back-office systems and not in an actionable execution layer. The notification center changes that by bringing together handoff reminders, parcel-linked alerts, reviewer actions, and supervisor digests in one operational inbox. The practical value is that the platform does not merely store process data; it actively helps institutions move work before deadlines slip.

## Slide 9 — Audit Integrity, Verification, and Chain of Custody

State that trust is one of the platform’s strongest differentiators. Signed audit packages, public verification, signature validation, key rotation, revocation tracking, and chain-of-custody timelines make it easier to defend the integrity of decisions and evidence. This is especially important in Nigeria where land disputes, extractives oversight, procurement scrutiny, and citizen confidence all depend on whether records can be trusted.

## Slide 10 — Sector Workflows for Nigeria

Explain that the same platform can support several high-value sectors without forcing each agency to operate in isolation. In housing and land delivery, it links title-adjacent processes, parcel context, and approvals. In mining, it supports cadastre-linked technical and environmental workflows. In oil and gas, it supports licensing, HSE review, and inter-agency coordination. The message is that this is a national capability platform, not a single-purpose app.

## Slide 11 — Value by Stakeholder Group

Address each audience directly. Federal regulators gain oversight, integrity controls, and a better national view of queue exposure. State land agencies gain operational discipline, service visibility, and stronger internally generated revenue potential through more traceable records and workflows. Builders gain a clearer path through approvals, less uncertainty, and fewer costly loops. Add that NGOs gain transparency signals and end buyers gain more confidence that land, title, and approval records align.

## Slide 12 — Economic Value for Nigeria and Africa

Conclude by linking platform capability to economic outcomes. Faster approvals can support housing delivery and infrastructure rollout. Better land certainty can improve formalization, confidence, and investment. Stronger extractives workflows can improve governance and sector credibility. Better evidence integrity can reduce dispute and leakage. State clearly that what makes this platform compelling is not simply digitization; it is the combination of **place, process, evidence, and accountability** in one system.

## Speaker Notes on Screenshot Authenticity

If asked whether the screenshots are real, explain that the screenshots used in the revised deck are real captures from the seeded application and were validated to avoid 404 pages or empty placeholder views. Also note that some geospatial visuals represent the **web parcel-intelligence surface**, while the full live map experience is intended for native device builds.
