# IDLR-PTS Procurement-Grade Presentation Script

This script is written for a Nigeria-focused procurement audience that includes **federal regulators, state land agencies, and builders**, with supporting relevance for NGOs, lenders, and end buyers. The tone should remain formal, strategic, and practical. The presenter should consistently frame the platform as **delivery infrastructure for land administration, Certificate of Occupancy progression, public revenue assurance, and trusted approvals**, rather than as software for its own sake.

## Slide 1 — Title

Open by positioning the platform as a procurement-grade operating environment for Nigeria. Explain that it combines parcel intelligence, land-rights workflow continuity, approvals, field operations, AI-assisted intake, and verifiable audit controls in one system. State that the goal is to help public institutions move from fragmented process management to accountable service delivery.

## Slide 2 — Nigeria’s Service and Revenue Challenge

Frame the problem in institutional terms. Explain that fragmented land and approval systems do not only slow service; they also weaken formalisation, reduce the visibility of land-linked public value, and make it harder for states to convert administrative work into transparent revenue and trust. Emphasize that this is therefore both a service-delivery and fiscal-governance problem.

## Slide 3 — What Government Is Procuring

Clarify that government is not procuring a permit portal alone. It is procuring a workflow backbone that connects parcels, legal progression, field evidence, AI-assisted document handling, role-based queues, and signed audit history. State that this matters because C of O and related workflows sit at the intersection of land certainty, service quality, and revenue capture.

## Slide 4 — Geospatial and Parcel Intelligence

Explain that land, mining, infrastructure, and right-of-way decisions are fundamentally place-based. The platform therefore links approvals to parcel identity, title context, geospatial readiness, and geofence-aware operational workflows. State explicitly that the geospatial visuals shown in the deck are **real web screenshots of the parcel-intelligence surface**, while the richer live map layer is currently a **native device experience**. This distinction matters because it protects credibility while still demonstrating geospatial capability.

## Slide 5 — Certificate of Occupancy Workflow Continuity

This should be one of the strongest slides in the presentation. Explain that the platform makes C of O progression visible from draft through review, approval, signing, and registration. Also mention continuity with related land-rights records such as Right of Occupancy and Governor Consent. For state land agencies, emphasize that this is where service quality, citizen trust, and revenue assurance become tangible.

## Slide 6 — Low-Connectivity Field Operations

Explain that a procurement-grade land and approvals platform for Nigeria must continue to function outside ideal connectivity conditions. Describe how mission control, secure offline caching, replay, reconciliation, and field evidence handling help teams continue boundary checks, corridor reviews, inspection activity, and evidence handling without collapsing when service drops.

## Slide 7 — AI-Assisted Intake and Document Review

Present AI as a throughput and quality-control layer. Explain that uploaded PDFs and images can be parsed to prefill forms, reducing repetitive entry and helping agencies process more work. Stress that side-by-side verification keeps the reviewer in control, and that provenance remains visible so officials know whether information came from AI or human input.

## Slide 8 — Queues, Handoffs, and Multi-Agency Governance

Explain that complex public delivery does not fail because forms are unavailable; it fails because queues, deadlines, and accountability between desks are unclear. The platform addresses this by exposing role-based queues, timed handoffs, reminders, digest summaries, overrides, and reassignment controls. For federal regulators, this creates stronger oversight. For state agencies, it creates service discipline. For builders, it creates more predictable execution.

## Slide 9 — Audit Integrity and Verification

Highlight the trust architecture. Explain that the platform can produce signed audit packages, support public-key verification, manage signing-key rotation and revocation, and maintain chain-of-custody visibility. Stress that this is particularly important in land administration, procurement scrutiny, extractives governance, and investor due diligence, where the credibility of records often matters as much as the decision itself.

## Slide 10 — Revenue Assurance and Monetization

Address monetization directly. Explain that states capture value through more traceable approval and registration workflows, better land formalisation visibility, reduced leakage, and stronger internally generated revenue assurance. Explain that builders capture value through lower delay cost, faster project conversion, and less wasted coordination effort. Federal institutions capture value through stronger governance signals, better sector oversight, and a more investable public-delivery environment.

## Slide 11 — Stakeholder Value by Audience

Address each audience plainly. Federal regulators gain a stronger national view of workflow health, audit defensibility, and sector risk. State land agencies gain visibility into C of O throughput, service bottlenecks, and revenue-linked workflow discipline. Builders gain predictability, clearer status, and lower carrying cost. Add that NGOs benefit from stronger transparency signals and end buyers benefit from more confidence that land, title, and approval records align.

## Slide 12 — Procurement and Rollout Case

Close by emphasizing implementation credibility. Explain that the platform has portable deployment readiness, a self-hosting path, native and web delivery surfaces, auditable trust controls, and a structure that can scale from state pilots into wider national or sector deployment. The key message is that this is suitable for phased public procurement because it can start with urgent land and approval problems while supporting future expansion.

## Speaker Notes on Screenshot Authenticity

If asked whether the screenshots are real, state clearly that the screenshots used in the deck are **real captures from the seeded application** and were validated to avoid 404 pages or empty placeholder states. Also note that the geospatial images shown in the deck are **real web parcel-intelligence screenshots**, while the richer live map surface is **native-map-dependent** and should be described honestly that way.
